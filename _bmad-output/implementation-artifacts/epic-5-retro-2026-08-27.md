## What Went Well

- **A lesson transferred *within* the epic, not just across epics.** Story 5.1 discovered the dev DB's seeded pickup slots had drifted into the past (`prisma/seed.ts` computes "tomorrow" relative to whenever it last ran) — a real trap, worked around by re-seeding, logged in `project-context.md`'s Actual State Log. Story 5.2's own Task 2 explicitly named this as the same decay class showing up in a different place — `schema.test.ts`'s hardcoded-past literal dates — and cited Story 5.1's discovery directly in its own References section before implementation started. The bug was caught by design, not by a failing test discovered mid-story.
- **Story 5.2's review caught the dev *introducing* the very device-clock dependence NFR2 was written to prevent.** The story's own Task 3 (discretionary) added a client-side pre-check comparing `startsAt` against the browser's clock — reasonable-looking UX polish that quietly reintroduced the trust-the-client problem AC #1/NFR2 exist to close on the reject side. Caught in review, not before; Jeff's call was to remove it entirely and rely solely on the server's `400`. A good example of a well-intentioned addition beyond the ACs still needing the same scrutiny as required work.
- **A deferred item that grew more consequential wasn't left to rot indefinitely.** Story 5.1's review flagged `PickupSlot.capacity` as unenforced and explicitly noted it was "now more consequential" since checkout started binding real orders to slots for the first time. Rather than waiting for a future epic to pick it up, Jeff made the call and it shipped as a direct follow-up (PR #9) two days after 5.1's review closed — same pattern already used for the deactivated-vendor dashboard-write gap in Epic 2.
- **Doc-accuracy discipline held for a second straight epic.** Story 5.2 caught `docs/api-contracts.md` still describing the endpoint as "Not currently called by any UI" — stale since before this story even started — and fixed it as an unlisted task, per `project-context.md`'s standing rule from Epic 3's retro.
- **Full-regression discipline after patching a review finding — Epic 4's own top action item — was applied and held in both stories.** Both 5.1 and 5.2's Dev Agent Records show a full regression re-run (not just the touched files) after applying review patches, with clean results each time.

## What Slowed Us Down / Recurring Problems

**1. A third instance of a story's own file/line reference drifting from reality.** Story 5.2's Task 2 opens with "`src/app/api/checkout/schema.test.ts` was a false lead — the file that actually needs fixing is `src/app/api/pickup-slots/schema.test.ts`." This is the same underlying pattern named in Epic 3's retro (line-number misattribution in 3.1) and Epic 4's retro (wrong-field citation in 4.1) — a third epic in a row where a story's own authored reference didn't match the file it needed to. Caught by the dev reading the actual file before trusting the citation each time, not by luck. Epic 4's retro already carries an open action item asking whether `create-story` should cite section headings instead of raw references for exactly this reason — this is now a third data point, not a new one.

**2. A known, now-more-consequential integrity gap sat live between the epic's last story landing and its actual fix.** `PickupSlot.capacity` was flagged as unenforced during Story 5.1's review (2026-08-25) with the review itself noting the stakes had changed. It stayed unenforced through all of Story 5.2 and wasn't closed until 2026-08-27 — two days, and one full story, after the review that named it as more urgent than before. Nothing broke in that window, but the gap was real and live in the interim. Worth a standing question for future epics: when a review finding is explicitly flagged as "more consequential due to this story's own change," should that trigger an immediate decision rather than a routine deferral, even when the finding itself is out of the current story's stated scope?

## Key Insights

1. **Within-epic lesson transfer is a distinct, valuable case from the cross-epic pattern retrospectives usually track.** Story 5.1's seed-data staleness discovery informing Story 5.2's Task 2 shows the same discipline (write down what you learned, cite it explicitly, act on it before it bites again) working on a tighter loop than "retro to retro." Worth reinforcing during story creation, not just during retrospectives.
2. **Deferring a finding and re-prioritizing a finding are different decisions, and a review should make clear which one just happened.** Story 5.1's review didn't just defer the capacity gap — it explicitly said the stakes had risen. That distinction is what let Jeff pick it up quickly afterward rather than have it wait for a future epic's scenario review to resurface it.
3. **A discretionary "nice to have" addition beyond the ACs is not exempt from the story's own stated principles.** Story 5.2's Task 3 was explicitly optional, yet still had to be reverted because it violated NFR2's spirit. Scope discipline applies to opportunistic additions as much as to required work.
