## What Went Well

- **Dedicated-fixture-over-shared-seed discipline propagated cleanly.** Story 1.3's round-2 review found and fixed a real test-race pattern (`.first()` clicks colliding with sibling tests mutating shared seed data under `fullyParallel: true`). Every subsequent story (1.4, 1.5, 1.6) cited that finding *before* a reviewer had to re-flag it, and used dedicated `createTestProduct`/`deleteProduct` fixtures from the start. Knowledge transfer within the epic worked.
- **Architecture deviations were documented, not silently "fixed."** Story 1.2 deliberately built `stockIsPlaceholder`/`thresholdIsPlaceholder` as dedicated boolean flags instead of the value-equality check AD-9's prose describes — a better mechanism than the spec literally asked for, because a vendor's deliberate `stockQuantity: 100` is indistinguishable from the migration placeholder by value alone. Story 1.6 explicitly called this out in its Dev Notes rather than re-deriving the wrong mechanism from the architecture doc.
- **The multi-round review process caught real regressions its own fixes introduced.** Story 1.2's round 2 found a fix from round 1 (Decision 1 + a patch) combined to silently break `thresholdIsPlaceholder` clearing. Story 1.4's round 2 found round 1's `stockDecremented` fix introduced a genuine concurrent-double-decrement regression under real load. Story 1.3's round 2 found round 1's `.first()` fix made a race deterministic instead of removing it. In all three cases, the bug was reproduced live (not just reasoned about) and confirmed to be a *new* regression by re-running against the pre-fix code. This is exactly why the process runs more than one round.
- **Story 1.6 turned out much smaller than planned** — its story-creation phase discovered Story 1.2 had already built the entire write-side mechanism (schema flags, migration, flag-clearing logic, tests) a full 4 stories earlier, in anticipation of 1.6's need. The actual Story 1.6 work was one read-only badge.

## What Slowed Us Down / Recurring Problems

**1. The stale Clerk auth fixture — the single biggest recurring cost of the epic.** First flagged in Story 1.1's review (10 failing tests), it grew every story that touched an authenticated route: 10 → 15 → 15 → 15 → 17 → 17. It was never fixed — every story just documented it, verified the failure signature matched, and moved on. `deferred-work.md`'s own entry already says: *"This is becoming a real bottleneck — every subsequent story touching an authenticated route adds more untestable coverage. Worth prioritizing before Epic 2 (all-admin, all-authenticated) starts."* That warning is about to come due — see Next Epic Preparation below.

**2. Accessibility gaps recurred even after being named once.** Story 1.3's round-2 review flagged and fixed a real a11y gap (native `disabled` with no screen-reader explanation). Story 1.5 shipped a *new* instance of the same class of gap (title-only tooltip, unreachable by touch/keyboard/screen-reader) and it had to be caught fresh in review. Story 1.6 shipped the *same* gap again — title-only tooltip — despite 1.5 having just been fixed for exactly this. Nothing in the process (Dev Notes, a shared checklist) carried this lesson forward automatically; each story's reviewer had to rediscover it.

**3. Documentation drift was found in nearly every review round.** `docs/api-contracts.md`, `docs/data-models.md`, `_bmad-output/project-context.md` all accumulated stale references across the epic (the dropped `isAvailable` column lingered in 4 doc files past Story 1.3's own code changes; Story 1.4's idempotency claims were overstated after round-1's own fix; Story 1.6's data-models.md needed correcting). Doc updates were consistently a *review finding*, not something the implementation pass caught on its own.

**4. Story 1.2's migration work needed 4 rounds to get right — 3 of the correctness bugs (shadow-database incompatibility, timestamp/timestamptz timezone mismatch, unguarded subquery cardinality) were only catchable by actually executing the SQL against a real database, not by reading it or typechecking.** This matters directly for Epic 2, which needs a comparably shaped migration (new `Admin` table, `Vendor` gains 4 new columns, two `onDelete: Cascade` relations get dropped).

**5. Two story files (1.1, 1.2) sat at `Status: review` — fully complete, every finding resolved — long after the work was actually done, because nothing flipped their status once the last finding was checked off.** Caught and corrected in this session's cleanup, not during the epic itself. Worth a lightweight habit: the last action in a review round should always be "does Status still say review with nothing outstanding? Flip it."

## Key Insights

1. **The review process's value shows up disproportionately in concurrency and migration correctness** — every genuine multi-round escalation in this epic (1.2, 1.3, 1.4) was triggered by either a real-DB-execution finding or a live-reproduced concurrency bug, never a typecheck/lint-level issue. Low-severity findings (styling, doc wording, dead attributes) were real but never blocking.
2. **A pattern named once doesn't stay named** — accessibility and doc-drift both recurred across stories despite being fixed earlier in the epic. Nothing in this workflow currently carries "we already learned this" forward except each story's own References section, which points backward at specific past stories, not forward as a checklist.
3. **Stories that build directly on a prior story's deliberately-over-built primitive (1.6 on 1.2's flags) come in dramatically smaller than the epic's own story list implies.** Worth reading the actual current state of dependency files during story creation, not just trusting the epic outline's story-size intuition.

### Critical path before Epic 2 starts

1. **Fix or route around the stale Clerk vendor auth fixture, and build the equivalent for a new Admin test identity.** This is not optional housekeeping anymore — Epic 2's e2e coverage is 100% authenticated by design (`getCurrentAdmin()` gates every `/admin/*` route per Story 2.1's own AC). Shipping Epic 2 the way Epic 1 shipped auth-gated tests — "confirmed the failure signature matches the known gap, moved on" — means Epic 2 could ship with *zero* verified e2e coverage on any of its 3 stories. `deferred-work.md` already named this risk before Epic 1 finished; it's now due.
2. **Real-DB migration verification discipline from Story 1.2's rounds 3–4 should be the default for Epic 2's migration, not a review-round catch.** The `Admin` table + `Vendor.deletedAt`/`createdByAdminId`/`deletedByAdminId` + nullable `clerkUserId` + dropping 2 `onDelete: Cascade` relations is comparable in shape and risk to Story 1.2's migration. Consider testing the migration against a real DB (or at least a rolled-back transaction, as Story 1.2's round-4 reviewer did) before first review round, not after.
3. **Carry the accessibility lesson forward explicitly this time.** Two stories in a row (1.5, 1.6) shipped the identical title-only-tooltip gap. If Epic 2 introduces any new interactive UI (admin forms, vendor-deactivation confirmation), check for this class of gap during implementation, not just review.
